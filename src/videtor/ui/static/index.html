How this works
  1. Shape and music: choose the frame (vertical, horizontal, square) and the music: your own track and where it starts, no music, or leave it to the plan. The cut always follows what you choose here.
  2. Reference video (optional): a finished video you want this cut to be like. Write the plan from this video copies its rhythm from your footage: as many shots, each as long, the same kind of shot in each place. No brief is needed then.
  3. Brief: paste it as the client wrote it (length, format, feel, what must be in and stay out) and save.
  4. The plan is the list of shots the cut fills: section by section, what each shot must show and how long it lasts. Claude writes it from the brief and the overview: copy the request below into Claude Code. When it is done, the plan shows here as a shot list.
  5. Check the shot list. To change it, ask Claude in the same chat, or edit it under Edit the plan. Then go to Cut.

How this works
  1. Watch the cut. Play it on the timeline, with its music. It shows stills until you press Load the moving picture (the footage drive must be connected; under a minute). Space plays and stops, the arrow keys step a frame, up and down jump from cut to cut.
  2. Change what you would change. Click a shot: use a runner-up, slip the shot earlier or later in its take, move the cut after it, look for more takes in your own words, or remove it. Every change shows at once, and Undo takes the last one back.
  3. Leave notes on a shot or on the whole cut for what the plan should do differently.
  4. Write the Premiere XML when it looks right, import it (the steps are in the box below) and finish in Premiere: grade, stabilise, titles. Effects and sound effects are in the XML, not in this preview.
  5. When the job is done, say how the cut was and save your finished video in the project's folder (at the bottom of this page). Videtor reads it and learns which shots you kept and which you changed; nothing to export.

Teach Videtor

How this works
  1. Every job you finish teaches Videtor. Save the finished video in the project's folder and it shows up below, ready to read. Videtor finds each of its shots in the footage and learns which picks you kept, moved or replaced.
  2. Work you cut yourself counts too. Give its finished video or Premiere XML, and the footage folder if you have it.
  3. Finished videos alone teach a style: pace, effects, the grade, how the cuts sit on the music.
  4. Rules in your own words are read every time a plan is written.
  5. After each of these Videtor learns, tries what it learnt on your past cuts, and keeps it only if they get no worse. Nothing here leaves this Mac.

New project

A project turns one shoot and one brief into a Premiere timeline.

How this works
  1. Plug in the drive with the footage. Nothing is copied or moved.
  2. Choose the footage folder; the name fills in from it.
  3. Create project. Videtor reads every take (faces, speech, what each shot shows) and sorts the shots by type: about ten minutes for a day's shoot. The project opens straight away, so you can paste the brief while it reads. A notification says when it is done.
  4. Keep the drive connected until the cut: the first cut reads in full the takes it needs.

Library

Sort a shoot into folders by shot type, and pull out every interview bite, without making a cut.

How this works
  1. Choose a footage folder and Add; add more when the shoot spans drives or cards.
  2. Sort the footage. Every clip is read and sorted into wide, close-up, talking-head, interview-bite and b-roll folders (links: the footage stays where it is). Clips with someone speaking are transcribed so their sentences become bites, laid out as a Premiere sequence.
  3. Check the sheets below. Wrong type? Change it in the clip's list (it moves the link), or move links between the folders in Finder.
  4. Learn from my sorting: from then on, every library and every new project sorts the way you do.

    Promos

    Give Videtor a finished video, as a YouTube or Instagram link or as a file, and get its best half minute as ready promos in the shape you choose.

    How this works
    1. Paste the video's link, or choose its file. A link is fetched to this Mac once; nothing is sent anywhere.
    2. Choose the shape, and what to make: Clips are the best stretches as they are; Edits are the best moments cut together.
    3. Make the promos. Videtor watches the video, listens to it and writes down what is said, then picks the parts. A ten-minute video takes a few minutes the first time; changing the shape or the kind afterwards is quick.
    4. Watch them below. Not the right part? Try another swaps that promo for the next best. Every promo is an .mp4 to post and a Premiere .xml to finish it from.

    Videtor picks by how the video sounds and looks, where a thought starts and ends, and (on YouTube) what viewers replayed most. It does not understand what is said, so watch before you post. Use videos you have the right to cut.
